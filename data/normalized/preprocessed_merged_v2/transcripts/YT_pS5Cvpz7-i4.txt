This new photo from the James Webb telescope is stunning. But that's not what space really looks like. If you flew out into space and looked at the pillars of creation, you wouldn't be able to see it with your naked eye. That's because humans can only see this tiny, itty-bitty piece of the electromagnetic spectrum, which we call visible light. A lot of those beautiful swirling gases don't exist in the visible light spectrum. They exist right here as infrared rays, basically heat radiation. And the James Webb Space Telescope was designed to see heat. NASA experts translate those infrared rays into visible light that we can see. Like this picture, where they took different pictures of one exoplanet in different bands of infrared light and assigned them to be purple, blue, yellow, red. It's kind of like translating a song from one key to another. Scientists assigned these crazy clouds of dust and gas to different colors of the rainbow to help us see them. The longest infrared wavelengths are red, and the shortest ones are blue. All the other wavelengths are somewhere in between, as oranges and yellows and greens. Put all of those translated layers together and you get this. An image that we humans can see and appreciate.
