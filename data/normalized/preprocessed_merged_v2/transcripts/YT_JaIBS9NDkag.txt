What happened to this gigantic sunfish? The sunfish is very rarely spotted, and when they wash up on Australian beaches every now and then, they get lots of attention. Scientists think that sunfish eat gelatinous organisms like jellyfish and zooplankton, and there isn't much chewing. The sunfish sucks the jellies in and out of its beak-like mouth until they're broken up into tiny pieces. Plus, sunfish have a special mucus lining in their stomach to prevent them getting stung by their dinner. And when they're not eating, sunfish also hang out on the ocean's surface, basking in the sun. This 2.5 meter long sunfish recently appeared on the Western Australia coast and its death could be linked to the severe marine heat wave. Ocean temperatures aren't the only threat, so even though female sunfish can produce up to 300 million eggs at a time, their populations may still be declining.
